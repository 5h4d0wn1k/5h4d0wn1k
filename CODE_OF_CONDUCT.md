# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Disagree with the work, not the person. Harassment of any kind is not
tolerated here.

## Why this repository has one

The subject matter is security tooling, so the people who show up here are
sometimes used to a culture where the sharpest thing anyone says about a
colleague's work is a compliment. This repository is a profile page with a
validator attached. There is nothing adversarial to win, and the tone should
match that.

The other reason: issues are open, and open issues are the easiest place for a
stranger to be unpleasant to a stranger.

## Expected behaviour

- Review the change, not the author. "This regex can backtrack" is a better
  review than a comment about the author's tone.
- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith, including when
  you are sure they are wrong.
- Say what you mean plainly, and say it in the issue rather than in a reply
  three threads deep.
- Take a disagreement to the code. If it cannot be settled there, that is a
  conversation to have directly, not one to have in public.
- Accept that a maintainer may close your issue or decline your pull request
  without agreeing with you, and that this is not a judgement of your worth.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, threats, or intimidation of any kind, public or private.
- Personal attacks, insults, or deliberately provocative comments.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing anyone's private information — a real name, an address, a phone
  number, an employer — without their explicit permission. Note that the
  maintainer's email and LinkedIn handle *are* published in the README on
  purpose; that is not consent for anyone else, and it is not consent for
  redistributing them.
- Sexualised or otherwise unwanted attention in a pull request, an issue, or a
  code review.
- Deliberately posting false vulnerability reports to get a repository's
  attention, and then walking them back.
- Sustained disruption — reopening a closed thread, arguing a settled point
  repeatedly, or posting to get a reaction.

## Scope

This applies in every space this repository owns: issues, pull requests, review
comments, discussions, and commit messages. It also applies when someone is
representing the repository elsewhere, and when someone is contacted as a
result of participating here.

The one extension worth stating: participants in the wider open-source work this
account does are expected to follow the contributing project's own code of
conduct, which may be stricter than this one. Where they differ, the project's
rules win, because they are the ones the maintainers there are enforcing.

## Reporting

Report it to the maintainer, either by
[email](mailto:nikhilnagpure203@gmail.com) or through a private
[vulnerability report](https://github.com/5h4d0wn1k/5h4d0wn1k/security/advisories/new),
which keeps the details off a public timeline while it is being looked at.

Include what happened, roughly when, and where — a link is enough. A screenshot
helps if there is one.

You will not be asked to confront the other person, and you will not have to
repeat the report to anyone else. Reports are read by exactly one person, which
is also the reason the response time is whatever a single maintainer can manage.

### What happens next

1. **Acknowledged.** Within a few days.
2. **Assessed.** The report is read on its merits. Being unpleasant is treated as
   unacceptable regardless of how technically good the contribution attached to
   the behaviour was.
3. **Actioned.** Usually a private word, sometimes a public one. Sometimes
   nothing, and you will be told that — silence is not a decision you have to
   guess at.
4. **Kept confidential.** Not published. Not forwarded anywhere, and not discussed
   in a public issue.

## Enforcement

There is one maintainer, so enforcement is also one person. That is a real
limitation and worth being honest about rather than describing an imaginary
moderation team.

In practice, the responses are, in rough order of how often they come up:

- a private note explaining why the behaviour was not acceptable;
- a public one, if the behaviour is already public;
- a commit being closed, or a contribution reverted;
- a block.

Two things are worth stating plainly. A block is not a negotiation and does not
come with a warning count attached — one clear enough case does not need a
pattern. And a block for conduct is not a judgement on the technical work: a
contribution can be excellent and still come from someone who should not be
unwelcome here, and those are separate decisions.

Retaliation against someone who reports in good faith is itself a breach of
this policy, and is treated as the more serious of the two.
